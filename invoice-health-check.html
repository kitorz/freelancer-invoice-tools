Free invoice health check

Most invoice rejections come down to a handful of missing or wrong fields — and a missing field can leave an invoice sitting in accounts payable for three extra weeks. Run through the 13 checks below before you send an invoice. Everything runs entirely in your browser: nothing you tick or type is sent anywhere, and nothing is stored.

Check your invoice

Tick each item your invoice already has. The score updates as you go, and the note under each unticked item explains why it matters.

  • Why it matters: without a number, accounts payable cannot file or trace your invoice, and duplicate numbers cause confusion.

  • Why it matters: the due date and any late-fee clock are usually counted from the issue date, so a missing one leaves both unclear.

  • Why it matters: without a deadline there is nothing to chase against, and the client has no date to put in their payment run.

  • Why it matters: the client matches you against their vendor records; a name or address that does not match can park the invoice.

  • Why it matters: an invoice billed to the wrong entity — a subsidiary instead of the parent, for example — is a common reason for rejection.

  • Why it matters: a missing purchase order number is one of the most reported reasons invoices get sent back; if the client uses POs, this field is not optional.

  • Why it matters: a single lump sum gives the approver nothing to check against the agreed work, so approval stalls.

  • Why it matters: totals that do not add up are an instant rejection — accounts payable will not fix your arithmetic for you.

  • Why it matters: terms tell the client when to pay and on what basis; without them, payment timing is whatever their default happens to be.

  • Why it matters: an invoice that makes payment hard is the most common reason invoices go unpaid — missing or wrong payment details mean the client cannot pay even if they want to.

  • Why it matters: a late fee you never stated is hard to enforce; stating it up front also makes the deadline feel real.

  • Why it matters: if something is unclear, the client needs one obvious person to ask — otherwise the invoice sits while they work out who to contact.

  • Why it matters: an invoice sent to the wrong inbox is not late — it is lost. A field can be perfect and still never be seen.

What this check cannot do

This page checks that the fields are present on your invoice. It cannot check that they are right:

  • It cannot verify a PO number, an entity name, or a billing period — those only exist in the client’s own systems, and a wrong PO or a name that does not match the client’s vendor records will still get the invoice rejected.
  • It cannot check tax rules, VAT IDs, or e-invoice formats. If you send structured e-invoices in the EU, free validators exist for that job (for example the Peppol validator).
  • It cannot fix the most common delay of all. Accounts-payable teams report that most late invoices are not bad documents — they are invoices nobody inside the company owns, and the approver is simply slow. A clean invoice helps; it does not guarantee a fast payment.

If the invoice is already sent and the due date has passed, the fix is chasing, not re-checking: the late-payment chase guide covers the 5-email cadence from three days before the due date to a final notice on day 30.
