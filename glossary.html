Terms A–Z

Accounts payable
Money you owe to suppliers or subcontractors for goods or services you have received.
Accounts receivable
Money clients owe you for work you have already delivered and invoiced.
Advance payment
Money paid before the work starts, often as a deposit or the first milestone payment.
Bad debt
An amount you have decided you will never collect, written off your books.
Balance due
The amount still unpaid on an invoice after any deposit or part-payment.
Change order
A written change to the agreed scope of work, usually with a price change attached.
Credit note
A document that reduces what a client owes, for example for a refund or a corrected invoice.
Deposit
An upfront payment that secures the booking and is deducted from the total when the job is done.
Discount
A reduction from the listed price, given for early payment, bulk work, or goodwill.
Due date
The date by which an invoice must be paid.
Estimate
A rough price given before work starts. It is not a binding quote unless the document says so.
Expense
Money you spend to run your business, such as software, travel, or equipment.
Fixed price
One agreed price for the whole job, whatever it takes to finish it.
Grace period
Extra days after the due date before late fees start. Leave it at zero if your terms have none.
Gross income
Total income before any expenses or tax are taken out.
Hourly rate
The price charged for each hour of work.
Invoice
A formal request for payment that lists what was done, what is owed, and when it is due.
Invoice number
A unique number on each invoice so you and the client can refer to it without confusion.
Itemised invoice
An invoice that lists each service or product on its own line, with its own price.
Late fee
An extra charge added when payment arrives after the due date, at the rate your terms state.
Line item
One row on an invoice or quote: a description, a quantity, a rate, and the amount.
Milestone
A stage of a project tied to a delivery or a payment, such as "first draft delivered".
Net 7 / Net 14 / Net 30
Payment is due 7, 14, or 30 days after the invoice date. Net 30 is the most common.
Net income
Income left after expenses. Tax is usually still to come, unless the figure says otherwise.
Overdue
Past the due date and still unpaid.
Payment link
A link on the invoice that takes the client straight to a payment page, so there is nothing to type in.
Payment terms
The rules on an invoice or quote: when payment is due, how to pay, and what happens if it is late.
Pro forma invoice
A preliminary bill sent before the work is done, often to confirm the price or secure approval.
Purchase order
A client's formal document authorising the work at an agreed price.
Quote
A fixed price offer for a defined piece of work, usually valid for a set time.
Rate card
A list of your standard prices for common services, so quotes stay consistent.
Receipt
Proof of a payment received or an expense paid.
Reimbursement
Repayment of money you spent on a client's behalf, such as travel or stock photos.
Retainer
A regular fee, usually monthly, that reserves your time or guarantees your availability.
Revenue
Total money coming in from clients, before expenses are taken out.
Scope creep
Work growing beyond what was agreed, without a matching change to the price.
Scope of work
The written list of what a job includes — and what it does not.
Statement of work (SOW)
A document describing a project: deliverables, schedule, and price.
Subcontractor
Someone you hire to do part of a client's job.
Tax / VAT
Tax charged on invoices where required. Rates and rules vary by country — check your local rules.
Timesheet
A record of hours worked, used to bill hourly work.
Value-added tax (VAT)
A consumption tax added to invoices in many countries. Rates and rules vary — check your local rules.
Write-off
Removing an uncollectable amount from your books, usually after the chase has run its course.
Work in progress
Work that has started but is not yet delivered or invoiced.
